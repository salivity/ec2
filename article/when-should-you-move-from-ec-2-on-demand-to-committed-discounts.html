When Should You Move From EC2 On-Demand to Committed Discounts?

Transitioning a workload from Amazon EC2 On-Demand pricing to committed discount models like Savings Plans or Reserved Instances is best done once your resource utilization stabilizes and demonstrates a predictable baseline for at least thirty to ninety days. While On-Demand instances offer essential flexibility for new, spiky, or short-lived applications, committing to a one-year or three-year term can reduce compute costs by up to seventy-two percent once your architecture matures and steady-state capacity requirements are established. This article explores the key indicators, utilization metrics, and break-even calculations needed to determine the exact moment to shift away from On-Demand pricing.

Understanding the Role of EC2 On-Demand Pricing

Amazon EC2 On-Demand pricing allows you to pay for compute capacity by the second or hour with no long-term commitments or upfront payments. It serves as the default starting point for most cloud deployments because it eliminates the risk of over-provisioning or getting locked into the wrong instance types.

Workloads should remain on On-Demand pricing under the following conditions:

  • Early-stage development and testing where traffic patterns are unknown.
  • Short-term projects, proofs of concept, or temporary environments.
  • Highly unpredictable or spiky workloads that cannot tolerate interruption (where Spot Instances are unsuitable).
  • Applications undergoing active architectural changes, such as migrating from monoliths to microservices or shifting to serverless containers.

Key Indicators That It Is Time to Transition

Moving to committed discounts too early can result in paying for unused capacity, while waiting too long leaves substantial cost savings on the table. Look for several operational and financial signals before making a commitment.

1. Stable Baseline Utilization

The primary indicator is a consistent usage floor. By reviewing AWS Cost Explorer or CloudWatch metrics over a 30-to-90-day lookback period, you can identify the minimum amount of compute capacity running 24/7. Even if your workload experiences daily peaks and valleys, the continuous baseline sitting beneath those fluctuations is a prime candidate for a commitment.

2. Right-Sizing and Modernization Are Complete

Before locking in a commitment, engineering teams should finalize right-sizing exercises. If an application is running on oversized instances, committing to that spend locks in waste. Additionally, if you plan to transition from x86 processors to AWS Graviton processors, or from EC2 to AWS Fargate or AWS Lambda, you should either complete those migrations first or ensure you select a flexible commitment model like Compute Savings Plans.

3. Predictable Business Continuity

Committed discount models require a one-year or three-year agreement. Before transitioning, confirm with product and business stakeholders that the workload will remain active and strategically relevant for at least the duration of the commitment term.

Evaluating the Break-Even Point

You do not need 100% utilization for a committed discount to be cheaper than On-Demand pricing. Because committed models offer significant discounts, they reach a financial break-even point well below full utilization.

Let \(R_{\text{OD}}\) be the hourly On-Demand rate, and \(R_{\text{CD}}\) be the effective hourly committed discount rate. If the discount percentage is \(d\), then:

\[R_{\text{CD}} = R_{\text{OD}} \times (1 - d)\]

In a given period of \(T\) total hours, a commitment costs \(T \times R_{\text{CD}}\) regardless of whether the instance is used. An On-Demand workload running for \(h\) hours costs \(h \times R_{\text{OD}}\). The break-even utilization ratio \(U_{\text{BE}}\) is the fraction of time \(\frac{h}{T}\) where both options cost the same:

\[h \times R_{\text{OD}} = T \times R_{\text{OD}} \times (1 - d)\]

\[U_{\text{BE}} = \frac{h}{T} = 1 - d\]

For example, if a one-year No Upfront Savings Plan offers a 35% discount (\(d = 0.35\)) over On-Demand pricing:

\[U_{\text{BE}} = 1 - 0.35 = 0.65\]

This means that if a workload runs for more than 65% of the hours in a year (roughly 15.6 hours per day or 238 days per year), committing to that capacity is more cost-effective than leaving it on On-Demand pricing, even though the commitment sits idle for the remaining 35% of the time.

Choosing the Right Committed Discount Model

When your workload meets the criteria to transition away from On-Demand pricing, AWS provides two main categories of committed discounts: Savings Plans and Reserved Instances (RIs).

Compute Savings Plans

Compute Savings Plans offer the highest flexibility and discounts up to 66%. You commit to a consistent dollar amount per hour (for example, $10/hour), and the discount automatically applies across any EC2 instance family, size, operating system, tenancy, or AWS Region, as well as AWS Fargate and AWS Lambda.

Transition to Compute Savings Plans when:

  • You have a consistent dollar spend across compute services, even if specific instance types or regions change.
  • You are actively modernizing applications from EC2 to containers (Fargate) or serverless (Lambda).
  • You want to minimize management overhead while capturing immediate savings.

EC2 Instance Savings Plans

EC2 Instance Savings Plans offer slightly deeper discounts (up to 72%) in exchange for committing to a specific instance family within a chosen AWS Region (for example, m6i instances in us-east-1). You can still change the instance size, operating system, and tenancy within that family.

Transition to EC2 Instance Savings Plans when:

  • Your architecture is mature and locked into specific instance families and regions.
  • You have no plans to migrate the workload to serverless or different processor architectures during the commitment term.

Reserved Instances (Standard and Convertible)

While Savings Plans have largely replaced Reserved Instances for general cost optimization, Standard and Convertible RIs remain relevant in one specific scenario: capacity reservations. Zonal Reserved Instances provide both a billing discount and a capacity reservation in a specific Availability Zone, whereas Savings Plans only provide a billing discount.

A Phased Transition Strategy

Rather than moving 100% of your On-Demand usage to committed discounts all at once, adopt a layered purchasing approach:

  1. Eliminate idle resources: Terminate unused instances and right-size over-provisioned workloads.
  2. Cover the rock-solid floor: Purchase commitments to cover 50% to 70% of your minimum hourly baseline using Compute Savings Plans.
  3. Monitor coverage and utilization: Use AWS Cost Explorer to verify that your commitment utilization stays near 100%, meaning no committed dollars are wasted.
  4. Layer additional commitments: Gradually increase coverage in increments as your confidence in the workload's long-term stability grows, leaving variable peaks on On-Demand or Spot Instances.
