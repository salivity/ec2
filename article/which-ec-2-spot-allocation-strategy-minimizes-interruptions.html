Which EC2 Spot allocation strategy minimizes interruptions?

Amazon EC2 Spot Instances offer significant cost savings over On-Demand prices, though Amazon Web Services can reclaim capacity with a two-minute notice when demand rises. To reduce the frequency of these interruptions when launching a pool of Spot Instances via EC2 Auto Scaling or EC2 Fleet, you should select an allocation strategy that prioritizes spare capacity stability. The most effective strategies for minimizing interruptions are price-capacity-optimized and capacity-optimized, whereas the lowest-price strategy increases interruption risk by focusing purely on immediate unit cost rather than pool availability.

Understanding Spot Capacity Pools

A Spot capacity pool is a set of unused EC2 instances sharing the same instance type and Availability Zone. Each pool has its own fluctuating supply and demand dynamics. When launching Spot Instances across multiple instance types and Availability Zones, an allocation strategy determines how EC2 selects and fulfills capacity from the candidate pools you specify in your launch template or fleet configuration.

If you allow your fleet to draw from \(N\) distinct instance types across \(M\) Availability Zones, the total number of candidate Spot capacity pools \(P\) is given by:

\[P = N \times M\]

Providing a larger value of \(P\) gives the allocation strategy more choices to find deep, stable capacity pools with low reclamation rates.

Allocation Strategies That Minimize Interruptions

Price-Capacity-Optimized (Recommended)

The price-capacity-optimized strategy is the recommended default for most workloads. It balances two critical goals: minimizing the likelihood of interruptions and maintaining a low cost.

When you use this strategy, EC2 carries out a two-step evaluation:

  1. It identifies the candidate Spot pools with the highest available spare capacity, which inherently have the lowest probability of near-term interruption.
  2. From that subset of high-capacity pools, it requests Spot Instances from the pool with the lowest price.

This approach gives you stability comparable to pure capacity optimization while preventing your fleet from unnecessarily landing on more expensive instance types when equally stable, cheaper alternatives exist.

Capacity-Optimized

The capacity-optimized strategy focuses strictly on reducing the risk of Spot Instance interruptions without considering price differences among your allowed instance types.

Under this strategy, EC2 analyzes real-time capacity data and provisions instances from the pools with the most available spare capacity for the number of instances being launched. This is ideal for workloads where the cost of interruption—such as restarting long batch jobs, losing state, or migrating big data nodes—outweighs small variations in hourly Spot pricing.

Capacity-Optimized-Prioritized

Available in EC2 Fleet and Spot Fleet, capacity-optimized-prioritized operates like capacity-optimized but allows you to define a priority order for your instance types. EC2 first optimizes for capacity to minimize interruptions, and then uses your priority rankings as a tie-breaker among pools that offer similarly high levels of available capacity. This is useful when specific instance families provide better performance for your application, but availability remains your primary concern.

Allocation Strategies to Avoid for Interruption-Sensitive Workloads

Lowest-Price

The lowest-price strategy provisions instances from the pools with the lowest current Spot price at the time of fulfillment, without evaluating the depth of spare capacity in those pools. Because inexpensive pools often attract heavy demand or have shallow reserves, instances launched with lowest-price face a significantly higher rate of interruption. This strategy is generally discouraged unless your workload is extremely short-lived and completely stateless.

Diversified

The diversified strategy distributes instances evenly across all defined Spot pools. While spreading instances across pools ensures that a single pool outage does not terminate your entire fleet simultaneously, it forces EC2 to place instances into shallow capacity pools alongside deep ones. Consequently, individual instance interruptions occur more frequently than with capacity-aware strategies.

Comparison of EC2 Spot Allocation Strategies

Allocation Strategy Primary Optimization Goal Interruption Risk Best Use Case
price-capacity-optimized Low interruption rate and low cost Very Low General stateless web services, containers, CI/CD, and standard batch workloads
capacity-optimized Lowest possible interruption rate Lowest High-compute jobs, machine learning training, and stateful big data clusters
capacity-optimized-prioritized Low interruption rate with instance preference Lowest Workloads that benefit from specialized hardware when high capacity is available
diversified Even distribution across pools Moderate Legacy fleets requiring strict distribution ratios across instance types
lowest-price Cheapest hourly Spot price High Ultra-short tasks or testing environments where interruptions have zero impact

Best Practices to Further Reduce Interruptions

  1. Practice Instance Flexibility: Include multiple instance generations, sizes, and families (such as m5, m6i, c5, and c6i) in your Auto Scaling group or EC2 Fleet configuration. Alternatively, use Attribute-Based Instance Type Selection (ABIS) to specify vCPU and memory bounds, automatically expanding the number of candidate pools.
  2. Span Multiple Availability Zones: Always enable all healthy Availability Zones in your target region so the allocation strategy can route around localized capacity constraints.
  3. Enable Capacity Rebalancing: Turn on Capacity Rebalancing in EC2 Auto Scaling. When EC2 emits a rebalance recommendation signaling that a Spot Instance is at elevated risk of interruption, Auto Scaling proactively launches a replacement instance using your chosen allocation strategy before terminating the at-risk instance.
